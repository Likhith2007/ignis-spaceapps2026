Chapter 01
Mission & Challenge Alignment

IGNIS is a production web application built for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026. It fuses NASA's two active-fire satellite record systems — MODIS (1 km resolution, flying since 2000) and VIIRS (375 m resolution, flying since 2012) — into one continuous, sensor-independent 26-year burning-activity record, then serves that record through a mission-control dashboard designed for the people who actually act on fire data: emergency responders, scientists, land managers, and firefighters.

The project targets Challenge #9: "Harmonization of MODIS and VIIRS Hot Spots" (sponsored by CONAE, Argentina's space agency, whose Spanish-language briefing and collaboration philosophy IGNIS follows). The challenge defines two explicit goals, and IGNIS implements both end-to-end rather than stopping at a visualization demo. Goal 1 — Data Harmonization — asks teams to make the two sensor records scientifically comparable despite their different resolutions, overpass times, and confidence scales. Goal 2 — the Web Application — asks for an interface where a user selects a region and date range and receives fire history, trends, anomalies, and downloadable products. IGNIS answers with a cross-sensor confidence cross-mapping, a footprint-area normalization, an overlap-derived continuity coefficient computed per region from up to 173 overlap months, and a unified time series that feeds every panel in the app.

The flagship deliverable is the Burning Activity Calendar itself: a monthly heatmap of fire activity stretching from November 2000 to the present, with each month scored against the multi-year historical median (a z-score), rendered with colorblind-safe color ramps, and directly clickable to load any historical month onto the live map. Around it, IGNIS layers early-warning triage, fire-weather forecasting, carbon-impact accounting, seasonal outlooks, and an on-device AI analyst — a full decision-support suite, not a single chart.

Chapter 03
Mission Control — Header & KPI Deck

The top of the page is a permanent mission-control strip. On the left sits the IGNIS flame logo and the one-line thesis: "One 26-year burning-activity record from NASA's split MODIS + VIIRS hot-spot archive — early warning for responders." On the right, a live UTC clock ticks every second, flanked by context chips: the challenge title chip (so judges never lose track of the alignment), a data-source chip summarizing FIRMS · GIBS · EONET · GDACS · ReliefWeb · HF, and two mode controls — EXPERT (toggles jargon-rich statistical labels for scientists vs. plain-language mode for the public, roadmap feature 7) and Tour, which relaunches the six-step guided onboarding tour (roadmap feature 6). An + AI Analyst button opens the AI panel from anywhere on the page.

Below the header, an EARLY ALERT banner states the current threat in one glance. At audit time it read EARLY ALERT · RED — Significant fire activity: 14,338 detections, 131,236 MW FRP, 10 conflagration-grade (≥250 MW). This banner is produced by a deterministic rule engine (not an LLM), so it can never hallucinate: threat levels step from LOW through MODERATE, HIGH, and RED as detection counts, total FRP, and conflagration-grade pixel counts cross fixed thresholds. Next to it, "Today in brief" delivers the one-line executive summary (roadmap feature 5) — counts, FRP, sensor split, and a hint to click any red dot for the full AI event card. A live headlines marquee then scrolls the newest named wildfire incidents (source: EONET), each stamped with its date and state or province.

The KPI deck renders six cards, all computed from the live harmonized pipeline for the selected AOI. LIVE DETECTIONS shows today's cross-sensor count (14,338 at audit). TOTAL FRP shows the summed fire radiative power in megawatts — the physical intensity of all burning. CLIMATOLOGICAL PEAK names the historical peak fire month for this region (September for the Amazon, with its Aug–Sep window). THIS MONTH VS HISTORICAL MEDIAN performs the calendar's signature anomaly comparison — the current month's estimated detections against the 2000–23 median for the same calendar month, expressed as a ratio and a z-score (audit: ×0.1 of median, z = −1.69, i.e. a quiet month). FORECAST PEAK (12 MO) projects the next twelve months and names the expected peak with the model's R² (Sep 2027, −212.0k detections, R² 0.812). BURN REGIME classifies the region's fire behavior against a prototype library (92% match to the Amazon dry-season regime).

Chapter 04
Region & AOI System

Every analysis in IGNIS follows a single region selection — one control drives all panels, satisfying the brief's requirement that a user "selects a region" and receives consistent analytics everywhere. Nine curated AOIs cover the world's great fire theaters: Amazon Basin, California / US West, Boreal Canada, Eastern Siberia, Congo Basin, Borneo & Sumatra, Australia, Mediterranean Basin, and — pinned for the builder's home audience — Bangladesh. Each preset carries its own bbox, climatology note ("Deforestation & drought-driven fire regime" for the Amazon), and per-region continuity coefficient.

Beyond presets, two free-form paths satisfy the brief's coordinate-input requirement. Pick AOI switches the map into drawing mode: the user drags a rectangle anywhere on Earth and every panel re-scopes to that exact box. The search box ("Search any place on Earth") resolves any place name through OSM Nominatim geocoding and snaps the AOI to the match. A Load 7-day window button pulls the trailing week of NRT detections in one action, and a Map date control sets the day shown on the live map — calendar cells and the search result list also set it, so the whole application stays day-synchronized. The selected region name and date propagate into the title of every analysis tab (e.g. "BURNING ACTIVITY CALENDAR — AMAZON BASIN"), making screenshots and exports self-documenting.

Chapter 05
Date-Range Search & Data Downloads

This panel is the literal implementation of the brief's Goal 2: "select a region and a specific date range, then receive fire history, trends, and downloadable products." Quick presets (7D / 14D / 30D / 45D) or explicit from–to date pickers define the window (capped at 45 days to protect the FIRMS transaction budget). Running the search fetches the harmonized multi-sensor hotspot record for every day in the range — FIRMS NRT as the primary source for recent days, the NASA GIBS vector archive as fallback for older windows — computes per-day, per-sensor, and total analytics from the pipeline's authoritative counts, and scores every returned point with the IGNIS fire-behavior classifier on the merged cross-sensor set.

Results appear as a KPI row — DETECTIONS IN RANGE (46,480 at audit), Σ FRP with mean MW per detection, PEAK DAY with its count — followed by a per-sensor merge table (MODIS Terra 1,890 · MODIS Aqua 7,063 · VIIRS S-NPP 0 · VIIRS NOAA-20 910 · VIIRS NOAA-21 36,617 at audit; the S-NPP zero is honestly footnoted: S-NPP has passed its designed sunset and its feed is degrading). An interactive bar chart of detections per day doubles as a day selector — clicking any bar loads that day on the live map. Three download buttons then close the brief's loop: Download detections (CSV) exports the deduplicated point list with sensor, confidence, FRP, day/night and coordinates (capped at 20,000 rows); Daily counts (CSV) exports the per-day/per-sensor analytics whose sums reconcile exactly to the totals; and GeoJSON (GIS) exports a GIS-ready FeatureCollection. A footnote keeps the science honest: historical windows beyond the FIRMS NRT horizon fall back to the GIBS vector archive, and the 26-year Burning Calendar tab is the authoritative historical record.

Chapter 06
Live Map Engine — Fire Rendering & HUD

The map is a maximized, full-bleed canvas rendering NASA GIBS WMTS imagery. The layer picker includes daily true color and the fire-focused 7-2-1 false color composite (burned areas glow), plus RainViewer radar/cloud overlays, nullschool-style wind streamlines, and a Windy-style opacity control. On top of the imagery, IGNIS draws the day's active fire detections with the custom BLAZE fire engine (v8): a single-canvas sprite renderer that paints each hotspot as a filled ember — proper red fill, glow halo, and a spread radius scaled by confidence and FRP — so weak detections stay small and conflagration-grade ones visibly dominate. A GPU-free isometric Fire Field fallback and a Leaflet DOM renderer guarantee the map remains interactive even on machines where WebGL is blocked (a lesson learned from an early GPUInitializationError crash, now gated behind WebGL2 capability detection).

Interaction is the point: clicking any fire dot opens the full AI event card — sensor, confidence, FRP, overpass time, day/night flag — together with the AI's plain-language read of the event, its threat badge, and the 🤖 verdict / 💬 deep-dive pair. A floating HUD chip rolls up the live state ("14,338 FIRMS LIVE · ⚠ 557 · 2026-10-28") and expands into a threat breakdown, while an ⓘ Legend popover decodes dot sizes and colors. Shortcuts, a cinematic replay chip for animated playback (roadmap feature 3), and map-date synchronization with the calendar and search panels complete the map experience. Responsive behavior is explicit: fluid map heights per breakpoint, mobile-repositioned HUD, and touch-polished popups.

Chapter 07
Early Warning & Event Triage

Beneath the map, a triage column answers "what matters right now?" The top block re-states the rule-engine threat verdict with its evidence — detection count, total FRP, conflagration-grade count — and is explicitly labeled as rule-engine output so responders know it is deterministic. Three authority feeds then stack below it. GDACS (the European Commission's Joint Research Centre + UN system) supplies official disaster alerts color-coded green/orange/red. ReliefWeb (UN OCHA) supplies on-the-ground disaster reports. EONET v3 (NASA's Earth Observatory Natural Event Tracker) supplies the open wildfire event list, ranked by relevance, each entry linking to the original event page with coordinates and date, with twenty live events at audit time, from the BEE HAVEN fire in Florida to prescribed burns in Arizona.

The 7-Day Fire-Weather Early Warning block (Open-Meteo, computed client-side; roadmap feature 2) converts the week's forecast wind, temperature, and humidity into a daily fire-danger score, names the peak-danger day, and translates the number into plain guidance ("MODERATE — typical burning conditions; normal vigilance"). A Region Context block grounds the numbers in geography with a Wikipedia summary of the region's fire ecology, and a "more" expander loads additional detail. Together these panels fulfill the target-audience requirement: a responder can glance at one screen and know the satellite picture, the official alert picture, the weather-driven risk picture, and the local context without leaving the page.

Chapter 08
Sensor Harmonization Panel

This panel is Goal 1 made visible for every selected day and AOI. It lists all five instruments — MODIS-Terra, MODIS-Aqua, VIIRS S-NPP, VIIRS NOAA-20, VIIRS NOAA-21 — each with its live detection count, overpass times (~10:30 and ~13:30 local for the MODIS pair), and its source stamp (FIRMS NRT API). The explanatory text then teaches the physics: MODIS images at 1 km resolution; VIIRS images at 375 m with a 3,060 km swath and resolves fires roughly 450× smaller in area, so raw VIIRS counts run far higher for the same fire. Comparing them naively would fabricate a fire "increase" that is really just a change of instrument.

IGNIS's answer is the continuity coefficient: during the 2012+ era when both systems fly, the ratio of VIIRS to MODIS activity is computed per region over all overlap months — 173 of them for the Amazon, yielding SNPP/MODIS ≈ ×14.724 — and that coefficient is applied when fusing the pre-2012 MODIS record with the modern VIIRS era, so one 26-year trend line is scientifically comparable across the sensor break. The panel closes with the day's top fire clusters on a 0.25° grid (cluster centroid, detection count, summed MW), which is both a data-quality check and a quick "where is it actually burning" answer.

Chapter 09
The Eleven Analysis Tabs

Everything below the map lives in a tab rail of eleven workspaces. Each tab re-scopes to the selected AOI and date, and each is described in turn here — this is the scientific core of IGNIS and where the brief's "calendar + trends + anomalies" language becomes concrete.

🔥 Burning Calendar FLAGSHIP

The brief's headline deliverable, fully realized. A month-by-month heatmap from November 2000 to the present (rows = years, columns = Jan–Dec), where each cell carries an estimated detection count, summed FRP, and a z-score against the 2000–23 median for that calendar month. Cells are clickable and load that month on the live map. Three display modes — Harmonized (fused record), Per Sensor (side-by-side MODIS vs VIIRS), and FRP (intensity view) — and three accessibility color ramps (Inferno, Colorblind-safe, Mono) are switchable in-place, satisfying the brief's "accessible color ramps" requirement verbatim. MODIS-era cells are stamped "MODIS-era scaled" for transparency about the continuity correction.

🌍 Impact · Air & Carbon ROADMAP #11

Turns FRP into consequences. Fire radiative energy (TJ) converts to dry matter burned and then to CO₂-equivalent via standard emission factors, reported in tonnes with relatable equivalents (cars-per-year, trees-per-year). The air-quality half probes Open-Meteo / CAMS for smoke-affected PM2.5 downwind of the AOI, closing the loop between "what burns" and "what people breathe."

🔔 My Fire Alerts ROADMAP #8

Personal alerting through n8n automation: a watchlist of regions plus detection/FRP thresholds drives a scheduled /api/hooks digest workflow that emails subscribers when their watchlist fires. The endpoint is automation-friendly (no chat-LLM dependency), so any n8n, Zapier, or cron consumer can subscribe.

🔭 Seasonal Outlook ROADMAP #2/#3

A 12-month harmonic forecast of monthly fire activity, decomposed from the 26-year climatology into seasonal harmonics plus trend, validated by R² (0.812 on the Amazon). The KPI deck's "FORECAST PEAK (12 MO)" card is driven by this model; the tab shows the full curve with confidence framing and names the expected peak month.

🔬 Methodology & Data DOCS

The transparency tab: pipeline description, every data source with version (MODIS C6.1, VIIRS V2), the harmonization math, classifier contract, and citation-ready methodology text. It exists so a judge or scientist can verify every number's provenance without reading code.

🧪 Fire Cluster Lab ROADMAP #9

On-device machine learning over the day's detections. A Hugging Face transformers classifier scores each merged point for fire behavior (flaming smolder, agricultural burn, conflagration-grade…), the classification contract being "per (AOI, day) across all sensors" so results are sensor-independent. Semantic triage uses a MiniLM embedding model running in-browser — zero-latency, no server round-trip — to group similar events.

📊 Daily Dynamics ANALYSIS

The diurnal story: detection counts and FRP split by overpass window and day/night flag, rendered as small-multiple bars. Agricultural-burning regions show strong daytime peaks; overnight burning shows up clearly against the Aqua and night passes. This is the tab that answers "when do these fires burn?"

📈 Time-Series Lab ANALYSIS

The harmonized record as a line: monthly totals with a Theil-Sen robust fit, a shaded p10–p90 band, and anomaly rings marking months beyond the band. Hovering exposes exact values; the same time series feeds the calendar and outlook, so all three views always agree.

🧮 Statistics Science Lab V8.2 · FLAGSHIP

Seven sub-tabs of classical, citable statistics computed live on the harmonized record, the deepest analysis layer in any Space Apps fire app reviewed this cycle. Trend significance: Mann-Kendall monotonic test (tie-corrected, normal approximation) with Sen's slope as the median of pairwise slopes — at audit: MK Z = −1.47, p = 0.1412, S = −64, slope −14.4k detections/yr, honest verdict "NO TREND, within noise" over 25 normalized years. Memory & persistence: autocorrelation function and R/S Hurst exponent (is fire activity a random walk or persistent regime?). Hidden cycles: DFT periodogram exposing periodicities beyond the annual cycle. Extremes & return periods: Gumbel extreme-value fit plus a Peaks-Over-Threshold register answering "how often a 2019-class megafire month?" Sensor agreement: Bland-Altman analysis of MODIS vs VIIRS paired measurements — the statistical gold standard for instrument agreement, beyond a plain correlation. Season phenology: onset, peak, and end-of-season timing across years as a phenology Gantt. Energy & clustering: FRP Gini coefficient (is burning concentrated in few megafires?) plus Moran's I and LISA spatial autocorrelation.

🧭 Burn Regime CLASSIFIER

Classifies the region's fire behavior against a prototype library of regimes (deforestation-driven, boreal-summer, Mediterranean-drought, peatland…), reporting match confidence (92% Amazon dry-season at audit) plus the regime's seasonality shape — unimodal vs bimodal, onset month, and typical intensity band.

🤗 Open Science ROADMAP #10

Status and links for the private IGNIS model family on Hugging Face (fire-type, fire-spread, fire-classifier, event-detector, footprint-forecaster, regime-model) and the harmonized dataset repositories on GitHub + HF. Everything reproducible, everything versioned, everything open where openness is allowed.

Chapter 10
AI Analyst Stack

IGNIS's AI layer is deliberately API-first and automation-friendly — there is no chat-LLM dependency anywhere. The bottom status card reads: "Hugging Face · MiniLM + Llama-3.1 analysis · Nabidnur", and the pipeline footer spells out the full stack. A deterministic rule engine produces every threat badge and the early-alert banner — it cannot hallucinate, so it is the layer of record. A MiniLM embedding model runs on-device for semantic triage of events with zero latency. A Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct endpoint writes the grounded event cards: given the rule engine's facts (counts, FRP, confidence, weather), it composes plain-language verdicts (🤖) and deeper explanations (💬) that never contradict the numbers. If the model is unreachable, the rule engine's template fallback keeps every feature alive.

The practical consequence for responders: the AI never gates the data. Every badge, banner, and summary has a deterministic twin, and every AI output cites the numbers it was given. This design choice — AI as narrator, not oracle — is one of IGNIS's strongest trust features and directly supports the challenge's emergency-responder audience, who cannot afford creative summarization in a warning system.
