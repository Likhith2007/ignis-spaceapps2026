SECTION 02 // RE-SELECTION UNDER THE NEW CONSTRAINTS
The AI-Native Re-Score: 14 Briefs, One Survivor

Same five dimensions, same weights as the dossier — plus one new adjustment: the AI-Native Solo Adjustment (ANSA).

The dossier scored all fourteen challenges on Data Availability (0.30), Demo Wow (0.25), AI Leverage (0.20), Story Power (0.15), and Competition Density (0.10). REV 2 does not retouch those numbers; it applies a single AI-Native Solo Adjustment between −1.1 and +0.5 that encodes what the constraint change does to each brief’s win probability. Three factors drive the adjustment: API-first data access (can every input arrive over HTTP without bulk local downloads?), CI-compatibility (can all processing run as scheduled Python with no GPU and no interactive tools?), and chat-AI buildability (is the code in territory where chat assistants are near-expert?). Briefs that needed local model serving or heavy interactive processing take the largest cuts; briefs that were already pure-API deterministic builds gain.

02.2 The Verdict, Re-Confirmed and Strengthened

The headline of the re-score is that the constraint change widens the flagship’s lead. PYROCHRON was already rank 1 at 8.30 under team assumptions; under solo-plus-chat it stretches to 8.80 because every one of its inputs arrives as a plain HTTP request, every one of its computations is a scheduled Python script, and none of its science requires a GPU or a served model. The distance to the field is no longer a matter of taste — it is structural. The fire record is the rare NASA dataset where the archive, the API, the documentation, and the update cadence are all simultaneously excellent, and the harmonization task rewards exactly the kind of patient, deterministic code that chat assistants generate reliably.

The bench reshuffles in the same direction. PULSE (#02) and ROOTLOGIC (#07) hold their pivot and alternate roles with improved scores — both are API-fed and statistics-first, and either can be executed by the same engine with a new skin. The CLPS browser (#04) is promoted to active reserve: its ephemeris math is fully deterministic, its competition is thin, and its data needs are the smallest on the slate. Dancing with the SARs (#06) drops from third to eleventh — not because NISAR is a bad mission but because a solo operator without local compute cannot bet the weekend on interferometric processing. SPHEREx (#12) likewise slides. The cold bench is now #08 and #14, kept for their AI-native and creative lanes respectively.
