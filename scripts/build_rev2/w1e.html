SECTION 06 // THE SOLO OPERATING RHYTHM
Managing One Body, Three Chats, Forty-Eight Hours

Solo execution fails by energy collapse before it fails by engineering. The rhythm is the plan.

A four-person team survives 48 hours by rotating exhaustion; a solo operator survives by banking sleep in advance and defending two hard blocks — roughly six hours in each night, non-negotiable, because hour 36 of uninterrupted work produces negative output. Between blocks, the operator works in 90-minute focus cycles against one session at a time, with the other two chats parked on long-running requests (a component build, a workflow run, a QA sweep) whose results are waiting at pickup. The commit cadence is the metronome: no 90-minute cycle ends without a green acceptance test and a push, so CI is always proving the last claim. If a cycle ends red, the rule is to revert to the last green commit and re-ask with a tighter prompt — never to debug past the second failed iteration in the same window.

Decision-making adapts to the missing team vote through hard default rules written now, while calm. Challenge commitment happens at the Hour 2 data check and only if a data check fails does the default order advance (PYROCHRON → PULSE → ROOTLOGIC); no swap is legal after Hour 2 under any circumstance. Scope disputes between the operator’s enthusiasm and the plan are settled by the plan — the bible’s portion list is the scope, and new ideas go into a parking-lot.md file rather than the build. The only metric that matters at every gate is: could a judge demo this in 90 seconds right now? Portion ordering guarantees the answer is yes from Hour 24 onward, which is what makes the final polish cycles calm instead of desperate.
