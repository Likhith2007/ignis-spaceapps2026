05 · Early warning
The right-hand rail

The rail answers one question continuously: is anything about to get worse? It stacks four live feeds plus the fire-weather verdict, and every one of them degrades silently to the deterministic core if its source is unreachable.

Rule-engine early warning

Zero-latency, deterministic threat assessment of live detections — no LLM anywhere in the path. It drives the same LOW → EXTREME badge vocabulary used on the map and in event cards, so a responder sees one consistent scale across the whole console.

EONET · ranked on-device

NASA EONET open wildfire events ranked by all-MiniLM-L6-v2 running in the browser (transformers.js/ONNX) for semantic relevance, then geo-filtered to the current AOI. The ranking model is local, so triage survives any server outage.

GDACS authority alerts

European Commission / JRC Global Disaster Alert and Coordination System wildfire alerts — GREEN / ORANGE / RED levels with severity in hectares, filtered to the region of interest.

ReliefWeb · UN OCHA

On-the-ground disaster reports from the UN's humanitarian news service, wildfire-first with honest context labeling when a report is merely adjacent to fire activity.

Below the feeds sits the 7-day fire-weather early warning card: an Open-Meteo composite of wind, humidity and precipitation trend for the AOI, resolved to a color-banded verdict label. In expert mode the rail also shows the Sensor Harmonization card — per-sensor counts and the live VIIRS/MODIS ratio for the selected day, the harmonization problem made observable in real time.

06 · Intelligence
IGNIS AI Analyst

A chat drawer plus automatic situation reports, grounded strictly in live telemetry: every prompt is built from the same API documents the panels render, so the model cannot invent numbers — it is instructed to cite only the telemetry in context and to refuse anything else. Two modes cover two jobs: chat ("is the Xingu basin active this week?") and sitrep, an incident-style situation report covering activity, trend, dominant sensor, risk outlook and recommended actions.

The conversational layer runs Llama-3.1-8B through the Hugging Face inference router with a zero-hallucination guard. If the endpoint is down or out of credits, a deterministic rule engine composes the same SITREP structure offline — the feature never dead-ends. The honesty contract is explicit in the UI: when the model speaks, the panel shows the model card; when the rule engine speaks, it says so. MiniLM triage (section 05) is the local component of the AI layer, so even with all remote AI down, ranking and the core analysis stay up.

Design rule: LLMs decorate, they never decide. Segmentation, analytics, classification and the early-warning engine are deterministic TypeScript; every AI feature has a rule-engine fallback producing the same output structure.
07 · The workspace
The eleven analysis tabs

Below the map sits the analytics workspace. Every tab is recomputed for the current AOI and day, and every tab names its data source. They are grouped here by job: the record keepers that answer "what happened", the deep labs that answer "what does it mean", and the horizon tabs that answer "what comes next".

The record keepers

🔥 Burning Calendar · the centerpiece

A 26-year × 12-month heatmap per sensor (2000 → today): rows are years, columns are months, cell color is detection count on the selected accessible ramp — Inferno, Colorblind-viridis, or Mono — with the choice persisted in localStorage and proper aria-pressed toggles. Months with |z| ≥ 2 against their own climatology are ringed; FRP bars carry the energy story alongside.

The "this month vs historical median" KPI expresses the current month as a ×-ratio and a z-score against the same-month median — provenance-aware for custom AOIs. The panel also prints the continuity coefficient: the median VIIRS/MODIS overlap ratio and its coefficient of variation, the honest uncertainty readout for the whole harmonization. Click any month to load it on the map.

🎯 Impact — carbon, climate & smoke

The carbon chain of the ignis-fire-calendar emissions dataset runs live: ΣFRP → fire radiative energy → dry matter consumed → CO₂e, with human-scale equivalences and context against the region's 26-year record. The smoke half pulls Open-Meteo CAMS air quality for the AOI center — US AQI band with advice, PM2.5, CO, aerosol optical depth — and flags smoke suspicion when the air column agrees with the live fire record.

🔔 Watchlist — personal fire alerts

Save the places you care about — home, farm, village — with a radius, and IGNIS checks the live detections against them, re-checking every five minutes while the tab is open. Channels are in-app notification, the Browser Notification API, and optional e-mail via a keyless relay (one-time activation, documented in the UI). There is zero server state and zero credentials — the watchlist is privacy-first by construction.

🧪 Fire Cluster Lab — segmentation

In-browser DBSCAN (ε adaptive to AOI size, grid-accelerated) plus convex-hull fire boundaries via Andrew's monotone chain. The cluster table reports ΣFRP, spread, hull area, front-ratio (perimeter ÷ 2√(πA) — elongation marks an advancing fire front), night share and sensor mix. A log-binned FRP histogram and a diurnal signature of UTC acquisition hours make the orbital detection pattern visible, and persistent fire systems are tracked across the replay window. Hulls draw on the map; clusters link to the Analyst.

📊 Daily Dynamics

The day-by-day picture: stacked per-sensor daily detections (the era split is visible at a glance), the fire radiative energy curve, day/night balance, and the MODIS↔VIIRS cross-validation scatter against the 1:1 line — the harmonization problem in one chart. A deterministic verdict closes it: ESCALATING / DECLINING / STEADY. The 🔮 next-day risk-field card from the footprint-forecaster model lives here too.

📈 Time-Series Lab

STL-style decomposition (trend / seasonal / residual) of the monthly record, a Theil-Sen robust trend, CUSUM change points, and a climatology view with a robust p10–p90 percentile envelope (replacing the old mean±1σ). Residual months with |z| ≥ 2 are ringed red (surplus) or blue (deficit) directly on the observed line, so anomalies announce themselves without reading a table.

The horizon

🔭 Seasonal Outlook  ·  🧭 Burn Regime

The outlook runs a harmonic (Fourier) regression forecast of the next 12 months in log-space with residual uncertainty bands, plus the event-detector model's P(extreme month) badge — a trained early-warning classifier over the 25-year calendars. The regime tab classifies the AOI by nearest-prototype distance against eight NASA-derived reference regimes, powered by the fire-regime model trained on 138k real FIRMS detections — "what kind of fire landscape is this?" in one badge.

📜 Methodology  ·  🤗 Open Science hub

The Methodology tab is the honesty panel: full data provenance (FIRMS NRT C6.1/V2, GIBS MVT archive to Nov 2000, GIBS imagery layer + date, EONET, Open-Meteo), the harmonization math, the MOD11 decode, and the S-NPP sunset note — VIIRS-SNPP decommissioning on Nov 2, 2026 and what it means for the continuity coefficient. Every limitation is stated where the user can see it. The Open Science tab fetches IGNIS's public Hugging Face assets live: the regime model (open weights, training script, metrics) and the 26-year dataset (long-format CSV, regime matrix, calibrated FRE→CO₂e emissions, complex inventories) with a datasets-server row preview.

08 · Deep science
Statistics Science Lab — 7 sub-tabs, 11 methods

An expert workspace for the "more scientific analysis" brief: eleven classical, citable methods implemented from textbook form in deterministic, auditable TypeScript — verified against 28/28 synthetic ground-truth tests that plant a known signal and assert the method recovers it. Each sub-tab is a distinct chart type and closes with a plain-language "Deterministic read" verdict, so a land manager gets the conclusion without reading the math.
